For the counterexample the optimal values are about y*(1/10) = 0.402031 and y*(3/5) = 0.900880; in the ε family the gap vanishes at ε = 0 and grows roughly like ε² for small ε. Between the cumulative points the polyline is a random mixture of two neighbouring cumulative tests; the optimal curve leaves the origin with slope equal to the largest eigenvalue of ρ⁻¹σ (about 4.216 for the counterexample, above P₁/Q₁ = 4), so on the segment between 0 and Q₁ the gap is larger (about 0.019 for the counterexample). ARGUED问题卷宗里的论证（未形式化）：对任意 t ≥ 0，使 tr(F(σ − tρ)) 最大的效应（σ − tρ 正谱子空间上的投影）在自己的假阳性处是 ROC 最优的；保真度本征基不保证每个累积投影都与某个 σ − tρ 的符号子空间对齐——这个反例里 σ − tρ 的 (0,1)、(0,2) 元是 (2 − t)/20 与 (1 − t)/20，没有一个 t 让它在坐标基里对角。ρ 与 σ 对易时，经典的阈值论证说明所有累积点都最优。Arguments in the problem dossier (not formalized): for any t ≥ 0, an effect maximizing tr(F(σ − tρ)) (the projector on the positive spectral subspace of σ − tρ) is ROC-optimal at its own false-positive rate; the fidelity eigenbasis does not force every cumulative projector to align with a sign subspace of some σ − tρ — in this counterexample the (0,1) and (0,2) entries of σ − tρ are (2 − t)/20 and (1 − t)/20, so no t makes it diagonal in the coordinate basis. When ρ and σ commute, the classical threshold argument makes every cumulative point optimal.

要判断一个量子系统处在 ρ（“阴性”）还是 σ（“阳性”），一个二元测量由效应 0 ≤ F ≤ I 给出：报“阳性”的概率在 ρ 下是假阳性 x = tr Fρ，在 σ 下是真阳性 y = tr Fσ。所有效应的 (x, y) 组成一个凸区域，它的上边界就是最优 ROC 曲线。Bodor 与 Koniorczyk 取 Fuchs–Caves 保真度观测量 M 的本征基做测量，按似然比 p/q 从大到小把结果依次并入“阳性”，得到 d + 1 个累积点，并问它们是否都在最优曲线上。

  1. 反例。Lean 证明答案是否定的：在三维里取 ρ = (1/20)[[2,1,1],[1,10,1],[1,1,8]]、σ = DρD、D = diag(2, 1, ½)，保真度观测量正是 D，第一个累积点是 (1/10, 2/5)；而 F = vv†/1382、v = (37, 2, −3) 的假阳性同为 1/10，真阳性 5539/13820 比 2/5 多 11/13820。
  2. 三维视图。球面上每个点是一个方向。在假阳性 x 处，最优效应由对偶斜率 t* 决定：它取 σ − t*ρ 为正的方向（青色）。白轴是保真度测量的本征基，琥珀轴是 σ − t*ρ 的本征向量；只有两组轴对齐时，累积点才可能最优。环是假阳性正好为 x 的投影，琥珀段比保真度折线更好。
  3. 两张图。左图是 ROC 平面：青色是最优曲线，白色是保真度折线。差距很小，右图把它放大：y*(x) − L(x)。ε = 0 时两者对易，差距处处为 0。

原文：问题卷宗 · FidelityMeasurementROCRefutation.lean · Blueprint · 文献笔记 · doi:10.1007/s10946-017-9629-5 · arXiv:1603.06801

To decide whether a quantum system is in ρ (“negative”) or σ (“positive”), a binary measurement is given by an effect 0 ≤ F ≤ I: the probability of answering “positive” is the false-positive rate x = tr Fρ under ρ and the true-positive rate y = tr Fσ under σ. The points (x, y) of all effects form a convex region whose upper boundary is the optimal ROC curve. Bodor and Koniorczyk measure in the eigenbasis of the Fuchs–Caves fidelity observable M, add the outcomes to “positive” in order of decreasing likelihood ratio p/q, obtain d + 1 cumulative points, and ask whether they all lie on the optimal curve.

  1. The counterexample. Lean proves the answer is no: in three dimensions take ρ = (1/20)[[2,1,1],[1,10,1],[1,1,8]], σ = DρD, D = diag(2, 1, ½). The fidelity observable is D itself and the first cumulative point is (1/10, 2/5), while F = vv†/1382 with v = (37, 2, −3) has the same false-positive rate 1/10 and true-positive rate 5539/13820, which exceeds 2/5 by 11/13820.
  2. 3D view. Each point of the sphere is a direction. At false-positive rate x the optimal effect is fixed by the dual slope t*: it takes the directions where σ − t*ρ is positive (cyan). White axes are the eigenbasis of the fidelity measurement, amber axes the eigenvectors of σ − t*ρ; a cumulative point can be optimal only when the two align. The ring holds the projectors with false-positive rate exactly x, amber where they beat the fidelity polyline.
  3. The charts. The left chart is the ROC plane: cyan is the optimal curve, white the fidelity polyline. The gap is small, so the right chart magnifies it: y*(x) − L(x). At ε = 0 the pair commutes and the gap is 0 everywhere.
