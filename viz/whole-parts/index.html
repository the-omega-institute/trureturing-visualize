两个量子比特，甲（Alice）拿一个，乙（Bob）拿一个。整体状态是一个 4 × 4 密度矩阵 ρ；甲只能读到自己那一半 ρ甲 = Tr乙ρ，乙只能读到 ρ乙 = Tr甲ρ。三维视图里，每个布洛赫球中的箭头是这一半的状态（箭头缩到球心就是完全混合的 I/2）；乙的球里的网格椭球是“引导椭球”：甲在自己那边做各种测量、按结果把乙的态分组后，乙可能被引导到的全部状态（Jevtic、Pusey、Jennings、Rudolph，Phys. Rev. Lett. 113, 020402 (2014)）。两半的箭头可以一模一样，椭球却天差地别——差别全在两边之间的关联里。

  1. 纯的整体，混的局部（观察者完备反射 §118）。贝尔态 Φ⁺ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 是纯态：秩 1、ρ² = ρ。可是任意一边单独看都是 I/2，完全混合（已冻结：bell_pure_state_has_maximally_mixed_marginal）。整体知道的，局部一点都不知道。
  2. 局部完整，整体仍不唯一（定理 118.1）。Φ⁺ 与 Φ⁻ 是正交的两个纯态（整体迹距离 1，一次就能分开），两边的约化态却都是 I/2（已冻结：bell_pair_local_global_residual）。对一边所有可观测量的期望都相同，当且仅当这一边的约化态相同（已冻结：local_observation_partial_trace_equivalence）；所以只看一边的测量结果，猜对的概率永远只有 ½；要把两边的结果放在一起对照，才看得到差别。
  3. 看不见的方向有多少（定理 119.1）。两个比特的迹零算子一共 15 个方向：甲本地 3 个（σi ⊗ I），乙本地 3 个（I ⊗ σj），关联 9 个（σi ⊗ σj）。一般地，m × n 维时局部方向共 (m² − 1) + (n² − 1) 维，关联扇区 (m² − 1)(n² − 1) 维，与局部方向正交，占全部迹零方向的 (m² − 1)(n² − 1)/(m²n² − 1)（已冻结：local_marginal_correlation_blind_spot）。页面右下的 4 × 4 网格把两个整体的差别逐个方向画出来：只要局部那一行一列全是零，只看任何一边都一点也分不开。
  4. 纠缠与经典关联。经典关联混合态 ½(|00⟩⟨00| + |11⟩⟨11|) 的两边也都是 I/2，Z ⊗ Z 关联也是 +1，和 Φ⁺ 一样；差别在 X ⊗ X 与 Y ⊗ Y 上（已冻结的同一条定理给出它与贝尔态的约化态相同）。Werner 态 pΦ⁺ + (1 − p)I/4 对每个 p 局部都相同，只有关联随 p 缩小（Werner，Phys. Rev. A 40, 4277 (1989)）。
  5. 应用：隐形传态（文献结果，本库未形式化）。甲把一个未知态 |ψ⟩ 与自己那一半做贝尔测量，得到四种结果之一，用两个经典比特告诉乙；乙按结果做 I、X、Z 或 ZX，手里的比特就变成 |ψ⟩（Bennett 等，Phys. Rev. Lett. 70, 1895 (1993)）。四种结果各占 1/4，与 |ψ⟩ 无关；不收比特，乙的态始终是他自己的约化态 I/2，所以不能超光速传信。用资源 ρ 做这套标准协议，对所有输入平均的保真度是 (2F + 1)/3，F 是 ρ 与 Φ⁺ 的重叠（Horodecki 等，Phys. Rev. A 60, 1888 (1999)）。不用纠缠、只测量再重新制备，最好只能做到 2/3（Massar、Popescu，Phys. Rev. Lett. 74, 1259 (1995)；Popescu，Phys. Rev. Lett. 72, 797 (1994)）。经典关联混合态恰好停在 2/3；Werner 态在 p > 1/3 时超过它。局部完全相同的资源，传态能力却可以从 1/3 到 1——只看一边读不到的那块关联，正是传态用掉的东西。理论卷递归关系观察 §165 用同一套标准传态过程模拟通道。
  6. 模型边界。所有态都是精确的 4 × 4（隐形传态时 8 × 8）矩阵；测量结果按玻恩概率抽样，A 与 B 用同一串随机数，比较才公平。保真度的球面平均按公式给出，测试用独立的数值积分核对它。抽样统计会涨落，精确值以读数为准。

Two qubits: Alice holds one and Bob the other. The whole state is a 4 × 4 density matrix ρ; Alice can read only her half ρA = TrBρ and Bob only ρB = TrAρ. In the 3D view the arrow in each Bloch ball is the state of that half (an arrow shrunk to the centre is the completely mixed I/2); the wire ellipsoid in Bob’s ball is the steering ellipsoid: every state Bob’s qubit can be steered into when Alice makes some measurement and Bob’s states are grouped by her result (Jevtic, Pusey, Jennings and Rudolph, Phys. Rev. Lett. 113, 020402 (2014)). The arrows of both halves can be identical while the ellipsoids differ completely; the whole difference lives in the correlation between the two sides.

  1. A pure whole, mixed parts (formal observer completion reflection §118). The Bell state Φ⁺ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 is pure: rank one, ρ² = ρ. Yet either half alone is I/2, completely mixed (frozen: bell_pure_state_has_maximally_mixed_marginal). What the whole knows, the parts do not know at all.
  2. Complete parts, an undetermined whole (Theorem 118.1). Φ⁺ and Φ⁻ are orthogonal pure states (global trace distance 1, distinguishable in one shot), yet both reduced states are I/2 (frozen: bell_pair_local_global_residual). Two states give the same expectation for every observable on one side exactly when their reduced states on that side agree (frozen: local_observation_partial_trace_equivalence); so from the results on one side alone the chance of guessing right stays at ½; only putting the two sides’ results together reveals the difference.
  3. How many directions are invisible (Theorem 119.1). The traceless operators on two qubits have 15 directions: 3 local to Alice (σi ⊗ I), 3 local to Bob (I ⊗ σj) and 9 correlations (σi ⊗ σj). In general, for m × n dimensions the local directions span (m² − 1) + (n² − 1) dimensions and the correlation sector (m² − 1)(n² − 1), orthogonal to them and making up the share (m² − 1)(n² − 1)/(m²n² − 1) of all traceless directions (frozen: local_marginal_correlation_blind_spot). The 4 × 4 grid at the lower right draws the difference between the two wholes direction by direction: as long as the local row and column are all zero, neither side alone can tell them apart at all.
  4. Entanglement and classical correlation. The classically correlated mixture ½(|00⟩⟨00| + |11⟩⟨11|) also has I/2 on both sides and the same Z ⊗ Z correlation +1 as Φ⁺; the difference sits in X ⊗ X and Y ⊗ Y (the same frozen theorem shows that its reduced states agree with the Bell state’s). The Werner state pΦ⁺ + (1 − p)I/4 has the same parts for every p; only its correlations shrink with p (Werner, Phys. Rev. A 40, 4277 (1989)).
  5. Application: teleportation (a literature result, not formalized in this library). Alice makes a Bell measurement on an unknown state |ψ⟩ together with her half, gets one of four results and sends it to Bob as two classical bits; Bob applies I, X, Z or ZX accordingly, and his qubit becomes |ψ⟩ (Bennett et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 70, 1895 (1993)). Each result has probability 1/4 whatever |ψ⟩ is; without the bits Bob’s state stays his reduced state I/2, so no signal travels faster than light. Run this standard protocol on a resource ρ and the fidelity averaged over all inputs is (2F + 1)/3, where F is the overlap of ρ with Φ⁺ (Horodecki et al., Phys. Rev. A 60, 1888 (1999)). Without entanglement, measuring and re-preparing reaches at most 2/3 (Massar and Popescu, Phys. Rev. Lett. 74, 1259 (1995); Popescu, Phys. Rev. Lett. 72, 797 (1994)). The classically correlated mixture stops exactly at 2/3; the Werner state beats it for p > 1/3. Resources with exactly the same parts teleport anywhere from 1/3 to 1: the correlation that neither side can see alone is precisely what teleportation uses up. The recursive relational observation volume §165 uses the same standard teleportation process to simulate channels.
  6. Model limits. All states are exact 4 × 4 matrices (8 × 8 in teleportation); outcomes are sampled from Born probabilities, and A and B share one stream of random numbers so the comparison is fair. The sphere average of the fidelity is given by the formula, which the tests check against an independent numerical integration. Sampled statistics fluctuate; the readouts give the exact values.
